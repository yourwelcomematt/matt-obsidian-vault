## Leather straps

* Leather quality
	* Look for a natural-looking surface
	* Order of quality from top to bottom is full grain -> top grain -> split grain (suede) -> genuine leather
	* If they mention the tannery it's a good sign
* Edges
	* Rolled-edge is nicer than cut-edge
	* Cut-edge straps usually have painted edges
	* Don't buy straps with exposed edges
* Stitches
	* More is better
	* Thicker is better - thinner stitches are often decorative
	* Different-coloured stitching on the top and bottom can indicate that it was machine-sewn
	* Should have double-stitching near the spring bar
* Lining
	* Look for traditional-looking calf lining
	* Should be comfortable
* Length
	* Comes in different lengths - I would probably need a small or regular length
	* Make sure the lengths of the pieces are balanced so that the watch sits correctly on your wrist
* Thickness
	* Thicker straps suit thicker watches, and vice versa

## Fabric straps

- Weave and density
	- Cheap nylon is thin, meaning you can see through it
	- Premium nylon is dense, silky, and opaque
	- Thickness is personal preference
- Texture
	- Woven
		- Standard textured strap
	- Ribbed
		- Harsher feel than woven
	- Tubular
		- Horizontal lines going across the width of the strap
	- Seatbelt
		- Shiny, which can be worse for keeping the watch in place but is less abrasive
- Edges
	- Plain woven edge
	- Melted edge - usually on cheaper straps
	- Salvage edge - different pattern on the edge
- Holes
	- Should be cleanly-cut and heat-sealed
- Hardware
	- Stainless steel
	- Thick, solid, wider (distributes weight better)
	- Branding
	- Floating second keeper
- Material
	- Nylon 6 is cheaper
	- Nylon 66 is stronger and more resistant to abrasion
	- Perlon allows you to set the clasp anywhere and lis light and breathable, but is usually stiffer
	- Elastic
	- Canvas
- Length
	- 280mm is the standard

Nylon expands when it gets wet

## Rubber straps

- Material
	- Natural/tree rubber
		- Very rare these days
	- Silicone
		- Softer, so tears more easily
		- Attracts lint and dust
		- Cheaper
	- NBR
		- Softer and more flexible than FKM
	- HNBR
		- More durable form of NBR
	- FKM
		- Best all-rounder
	- Polyurethane
		- More plasticky than rubbery
