## Major

### I, V, flat VII, IV

- Flat VII is from the Mixolydian mode, which is the same as a major scale except with a flattened 7th degree, giving a cooler and more mellow sound
- Works because I to V is effectively the same movement as flat VII to IV

### Flat VI, flat VII, I

- Feels like you're in the minor key until the last chord - called a Picardy Third

### vi, II, V, I

- Circle of Fifths chord progression
- Can use extensions e.g., Am7, D9, G11, C
	- G11 is the same as an F triad over G 

### I, V, ii, IV

- ii and IV have sub-dominant function
- E.g., Closing Time

### I, VI, vi, V

- E.g., Mr Brightside

### I, III, vi, IV

- III functions as the V of vi (secondary dominant - V of any other chord besides I)
- E.g., All My Favorite Songs

### ii, V, I

- Can be used to change to any target key by using that key's ii and V
	- Ideally, the ii would be somewhat related to the preceding chord in order to make the key change smoother
- ii and I can be major, minor, dominant seventh, or diminished, as long as the V is there

### I/V, V, I

- Cadential 6/4 (indicate second inversion)
- Used at the end of a chord progression

### Special Chords

- 4 over 5
	- Used in place of the 5 to resolve back to the 1
- 5 over 4
	- Used in place of the 4
- 5 over 6
	- Used in place of the 6

### Tritone substitution

- Where the dominant chord (V7 for major) is substituted with the chord that's a tritone (three tones) away
- E.g., in the key of C, G7 is replaced by D flat 7
- Works because the existing tritone in the dominant chord is preserved in the tritone substitution
- This tritone is what creates the tension that makes it feel like the chord wants to resolve to the tonic chord
- Can also tritone sub the VI7 chord
- Can also tritone sub the secondary dominants (i.e., the dominant chords of chords in the key)


## Minor

### i, flat II

- Flat II is from the Phrygian mode
- Gives an eerier sound

### i, VI, V

- V comes from the harmonic minor scale, which has a raised 7th degree - would normally be a minor chord

### i, IV

- IV comes from the Dorian mode, which has a raised 6th degree - would normally be a minor chord

### i, III, VII, IV
- Plagal Cascade, where we're descending by a perfect 4th each time (Plagal Cadence)
- E.g., Wonderwall, Boulevard

### i, VII, v, VI
- E.g., Can't Stop

### i, VII, VI, V
- Andalusian Cadence
