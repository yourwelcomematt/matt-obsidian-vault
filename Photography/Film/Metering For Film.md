## Film Density

- Density is a measure of how dark an exposed image is, which corresponds to how much detail is recorded on the film
- For negative film:
	- When light hits the film, it reacts with the silver halide crystals in the emulsion and causes them to retain a latent image
	- When the film is developed, the silver that didn't receive as much light (or any light at all) is washed away, while the silver that did receive light is retained
	- This results in the areas of the image that received more light being thicker and darker (i.e., denser), and areas of the image that received less light being thinner and lighter
	- Because of this, it is best to lean towards overexposure if you're unsure what the correct exposure is, as this will produce denser images where shadow detail is retained and there's detail in the highlights
- For slide film:
	- When light hits the film, it reacts with the silver halide crystals in the emulsion and causes them to retain a latent image
	- When the film is developed, the silver that received more light is washed away, while the silver that didn't receive light is retained
	- This results in the areas of the image that received less light being thicker and darker - i.e., denser
	- Because of this, it is best to lean towards underexposure if you're not sure what the correct exposure is, as this will produce denser images where the the highlights aren't blown out and there's detail in the shadows
