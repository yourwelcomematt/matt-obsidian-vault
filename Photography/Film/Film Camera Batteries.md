## PX625/PX13/MR9
- Used by most cameras from the 60s and earlier (e.g., Pentax Spotmatic, Canonet)
- 1.35v with very stable voltage discharge and long battery life
- Banned due to mercury toxicity
## Zinc Air (Wein Cell)
- Direct modern replacement for PX625/PX13/MR9 due to 1.35v discharge
- Main downside is short battery life, as once the pull tab is removed, the chemical reaction continues until it's finished
- Also relatively expensive
## PX625A
- Modern alkaline version of PX625/PX13/MR9
- 1.5v instead of 1.35v, meaning it can give inaccurate readings in cameras designed for mercury batteries
- Some cameras are designed to compensate for the voltage difference and give accurate readings, such as the Pentax Spotmatics
## LR44
- Alkaline battery used by most cameras from the 70s and early 80s (e.g., Nikon FE, F2, Pentax MX)
- Common and cheap
- Voltage discharge drops off gradually over time, resulting in inaccurate readings over time
## SR44
- Silver oxide version of LR44
- Better than LR44 as the voltage discharge is constant over time, meaning it will give accurate readings right up until it dies
